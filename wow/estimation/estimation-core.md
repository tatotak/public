# Estimation core

## Mindset

- **Estimates are predictions, not promises.**
- **Estimates are relative.** Compare work to other work.
- **Estimates get more accurate over time - expect to revise them.**

## How to estimate

### Break work down

- **Estimate tasks, not milestones.** Small, specific tasks are easier to estimate. Split epics
  first.
- **Decompose large work** into smaller, clearer chunks.
- **Any task >= 8 SP lacks information - spike first, then split.**

### Estimate as a team

- **More perspectives → more accurate estimates.** Uncovers risks, dependencies and hidden work.

### Use buckets and relative size

- **Use buckets, not raw numbers.** Fibonacci (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) or T-shirt sizes. Buckets make
  comparison easier
- **Size relative to other backlog items.** "What is this like? Bigger or smaller?"

### If unsure, pick the bigger bucket

- **Lean conservative - delivering early is easier than explaining delays.**

## Flow

- **Stop starting, start finishing.** Finish work in progress before picking up new tasks.
- **Carryover distorts velocity.** Rescope during planning or split into done vs left.
- **Don't plan to full capacity - leave a buffer** for sickness, holidays and unplanned work.
- **Use spare time to finish, not to start.** Pair, review, help others, pay off tech debt.
- **Pair up to go faster.** Two focused people beat back-and-forth across channels. Pairing with a
  mentor is a great way to learn.
- **Come with an open PR, not a meeting.** A concrete change to discuss settles questions faster
  than meetings.
- **Review together.** Mob reviews beat long comment threads.

## Limit rework

- **Changes are cheapest early.**
- **Research, design and clarify requirements before committing.**

## Cone of uncertainty

- **Estimates are least accurate at the start.**
- **Review and update estimates regularly.**
- **Make uncertainty visible** in plans.
- **Be conservative early** - early assumptions are often wrong.
- **Involve multiple functions early.**

## Accuracy vs precision

- **Accuracy** - how close to reality. **Precision** - how consistent.
- **Accuracy over precision.** Roughly right beats precisely wrong.
- **Consistent relative sizing makes forecasts accurate over time.** Velocity corrects consistent
  bias, not random sizing.

Ways to improve accuracy:

- Keep code **simple and predictable**.
- Use **relative estimation** instead of time-based guesses.
- Seek **expert opinion** for tricky areas.
- **Avoid bias** (anchoring, optimism).
- Include **multiple perspectives**.
- **Share knowledge** openly.

## Track every sprint

- **Velocity is a forecast, not a target.** Don't set velocity goals or compare teams - it leads to
  point inflation.

Review in the retro:

- **Planned vs completed SP**
- **Carryover** - what and why
- **Estimate misses** - what and why
